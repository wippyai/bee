# Gateway hooks

Proposed 2026-09-09 for Astra's review (round 61 asked for a compact contract before any code; rounds 62, 63 and 64 amended it). Implemented 2026-09-10: the endpoint adapters (`src/gateway/hooks.lua`, `hook_http_method.lua`, `hook_mcp_method.lua`, migration 5), the intake lifecycle (migration 6, `hook_claim`, `hook_ack`, `hook_reject`), the carrier integration (launch policy `gateway_hooks`, `bee.harness.carrier:machine` `drain_hooks`, records `bee.harness.hook`), the placement projection of both harnesses' hook adapters and the hook credential, and the proofs (`tests/gateway.py`, `tests/lua/gateway/hooks_test.lua`, `tests/lua/harness/gateway_carrier_test.lua`, `tests/lua/harness/gateway_harness_test.lua`).

## What exists

`POST /hook/{action}` takes Claude Code's http hook form (the event JSON as posted) and `POST /hook/{action}/mcp` serves Codex's `mcp_tool` form (one tool, `hook`); `GET /hook/{action}/{event_id}` answers a submission's status. Both forms authenticate a hook credential: `admit` takes `hooks` (event names from the closed catalog) and `materialize` then mints two credentials of one generation, `token` (kind tool) and `hook_token` (kind hook); `authenticate` refuses a credential on the other kind's endpoint. A submission is normalized to allowlisted fields and queued in the gateway store (`bee_gateway_hooks`, status `queued`) under the binding's identity; `bee.gateway:hook_queue` lists a binding's queue for the carrier that will commit it and for proofs. Answers to the harness: http form 202 (queued) or 200 (committed) with an empty body and the event id in `X-Bee-Event`; every refusal is a status with a `text/plain` body (401, 403, 400, 409, 413, 429 with `Retry-After`, 503), never JSON; MCP form a bare text result `queued <event_id>` (the status word, `committed` once the carrier has committed it) or a JSON-RPC error. The queue is a durable intake in the gateway store (Astra round 64 accepted it; the http handlers are stateless function executions with no shared memory): 202 means durably accepted by the gateway and not committed to the thread; a status query answers `queued`, `committed` (a thread record exists) or `rejected` (terminally, never to be committed), and `unknown` only for an id the binding never held or one past the retention horizon. The carrier drains the queue as the contract below defines; an intake is committed only when the carrier's thread commit of it is acknowledged.

## Intake lifecycle (contract for carrier integration, proof 5)

| Rule | Definition |
|---|---|
| Seal and revoke | Two operations. `seal` ends intake: from its point, which is the same transaction every submission checks (authentication, replay lookup, seal check, bound and insert are one transaction), no submission is accepted (403 `intake is sealed`), while the accepted rows stay for bounded draining and the credentials stay valid. `revoke` invalidates the credentials and rejects only rows no carrier claimed (`binding revoked`). A claimed row may already be in the thread when its acknowledgment is lost, so it remains queued for reconciliation. The runner seals when the child exits; the carrier at close seals, drains until the queue is empty or the host's `drain_ms` budget is spent, rejects unclaimed rows explicitly (`attempt settled`), then revokes. Carrier loss without takeover (`revoke_attempt`), supersession, expiry and a listener epoch change likewise reject only unclaimed rows. |
| Claim | The carrier drains its binding's queue through `hook_claim {binding_id, carrier_epoch, limit}` (on a one-second tick, on open or resume, once more at settlement before the turn ends, and at close after the seal): queued rows are marked claimed by that epoch (step `hooks_claimed`); rows claimed by a lower epoch are taken over (a lost carrier's claims), rows claimed by a higher epoch are never touched. A claim whose request check finds its epoch below the highest admitted epoch is refused. A sealed binding's queue is still its carrier's to drain. After revocation, expiry or a listener epoch change, the internally authorized claim operation rejects unclaimed rows, then may reclaim only already-claimed rows; it mints no credential and accepts no new hook. |
| Commit | The carrier commits each claimed submission as an `observation` record `bee.harness.hook` (payload: event id, event, occurrence, ambiguity, provenance, sequence, binding id and the allowlisted fields, canonically encoded and free of anything of the committing carrier) through its own commit path together with its checkpoint, in the same thread transaction as every other record it writes; the record's event key is `hook:<binding>:<event>:<occurrence>` for an unambiguous occurrence and `hook:<event_id>` for an ambiguous one, so the thread replays a second commit of the same submission and refuses a different body under the same key. |
| Acknowledge | After the thread commit the carrier calls `hook_ack {binding_id, carrier_epoch, event_ids}`; only the epoch that claimed a row may acknowledge it, and acknowledgment marks the row `committed`. A replacement therefore takes a row over at its own claim, commits it, and acknowledges it; an original that continues after being fenced acknowledges nothing (its acknowledgment matches no row it still holds) and its commit is refused by the thread. A crash after the thread commit and before the acknowledgment leaves rows claimed; the replacement re-claims and re-commits them, and the thread's event key deduplicates. A crash before the thread commit leaves nothing behind but the claim, which the replacement takes over. |
| End of intake | At settlement the carrier drains the queue once more before the turn ends and the receipt is written; at close it seals, drains within `drain_ms`, rejects unclaimed rows (`attempt settled`) and revokes the binding, so the endpoint refuses from then on. Supersession by a later carrier epoch and placement's revocation of a lost carrier's attempt reject unclaimed rows (`binding superseded`, `binding revoked`); expiry and a listener epoch change do the same at the next claim. A claimed row stays `queued` with `claimed_epoch > 0` until a carrier acknowledges a known thread commit. That is a durable uncertainty, not a promise it can still be committed: after an attempt has ended no carrier operation can append it, so the row can persist indefinitely for inspection. Nothing is ever marked committed that no carrier committed, and nothing a hook says settles a turn or extends an ended attempt. Events a harness emits only while shutting down (`SessionEnd`, `StopFailure`) are not admitted by generated configuration in this version and placement capabilities say so (`gateway.shutdown_events_not_admitted`); the endpoint still catalogs them for a submission that arrives before the seal. |
| Retention | Per binding at most 64 queued rows (429 past it), including claimed-but-unacknowledged rows, and at most 256 committed or rejected rows and 512 KiB of stored fields; the oldest committed rows past the bound are pruned, which is the replay and status horizon: a replay of a pruned occurrence queues again and its record deduplicates at the thread by event key, and its status answers `unknown` until then. A retained claimed row is never pruned automatically because pruning or rejecting it would assert a commit outcome the gateway cannot prove. | Hooks are observations a harness child reports about itself over the gateway. They grant no execution or approval authority, settle no turn, and never become a second record writer: every hook record is committed by the fenced carrier through its own commit path.

The gateway checks the highest admitted epoch and applies each claim, acknowledgment or rejection in one gateway-store transaction, serialized with admission. That transaction is separate from the thread commit, so a gateway claim is never proof of a commit. The thread carrier epoch remains the fence for the record write; gateway row predicates prevent a stale acknowledgment after a replacement has reclaimed that row, and a replacement reconciles retained claims through the thread's stable event key.

## Sources, from actual captures

Both harnesses were run with recording hooks (Claude Code 2.1.267 from `~/.claude/settings.json` command hooks; Codex 0.153.4 from `$CODEX_HOME/hooks.json` under `--dangerously-bypass-hook-trust`) around a scripted turn that called the gateway's `thread_read`. Six events fired in both: `SessionStart`, `UserPromptSubmit`, `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse`, `Stop`, `SessionEnd`. Payloads as captured (paths and ids elided):

| Event | Claude Code fields | Codex fields |
|---|---|---|
| common | `session_id`, `transcript_path`, `cwd`, `hook_event_name`, `prompt_id` (after the first prompt), `permission_mode`, `effort` (tool events) | `session_id`, `turn_id` (turn events), `transcript_path`, `cwd`, `hook_event_name`, `model`, `permission_mode` |
| `SessionStart` | `source: startup` | `source: startup` |
| `UserPromptSubmit` | `prompt` | `prompt` |
| `PreToolUse` | `tool_name`, `tool_input`, `tool_use_id` | `tool_name` (`mcp__bee__thread_read` for the gateway tool), `tool_input`, `tool_use_id` |
| `PostToolUse` | as above plus `tool_response`, `duration_ms` | as above plus `tool_response` (`{content, isError}`) |
| `Stop` | `stop_hook_active`, `last_assistant_message`, `background_tasks`, `session_crons` | `stop_hook_active`, `last_assistant_message` |
| `SessionEnd` | `reason` | `reason` |

Documented but not captured, and outside the first slice: Claude Code `PostToolUseFailure`, `StopFailure` (`error`, `error_details`), `Notification`, `SubagentStart`/`SubagentStop`, compaction and model-switch events; Codex `PermissionRequest`, `PreCompact`/`PostCompact`, `SubagentStart`/`SubagentStop`, `Interrupt`. Claude Code's reference states `Stop` does not fire on a user interrupt or an API error (`StopFailure` fires instead) and that on SIGTERM only `SessionEnd` runs; whether `Stop` fires on `max_turns` is not documented. These are why no hook may stand for a terminal outcome.

## Delivery, per harness, through host-rendered configuration only

| Harness | Mechanism (proven against Claude Code 2.1.267) | What the host renders |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Code | `type: "http"` hook handlers POST the event JSON to a URL with the header interpolating `${BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN}` when the handler's `allowedEnvVars` names it (the settings-level `httpHookAllowedEnvVars` was not required in user scope; both are still set). Observed: `UserPromptSubmit`, `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse`, `Stop` and `SessionEnd` arrive over http; `SessionStart` does not (it fires for command hooks only in this version). Timing: each hook is synchronous and bounded by its `timeout` (2 s configured); a slow endpoint costs at most the timeout per hook and the turn proceeds ("Hook cancelled"); an HTTP 500 costs nothing and the turn proceeds; an absent endpoint costs the connect attempts up to the timeout per hook (7 s for three hooks at 2 s) and the turn proceeds. Authority: a JSON body carrying `continue: false` or `decision: block` on `UserPromptSubmit` blocked the prompt and the turn never reached `Stop`, so the harness does honor decisions from an http answer; the gateway must answer every hook with 2xx and an empty body, never with a body. | `.claude/settings.json` in the private HOME, a generated hook configuration adapter carrying only: the admitted events, each with one http handler to `http://127.0.0.1:18790/hook/{action}`, `headers.Authorization = "Bearer ${BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN}"`, `allowedEnvVars = ["BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN"]`, `timeout = 2`; `allowedHttpHookUrls = [that URL]`; `httpHookAllowedEnvVars = ["BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN"]`. Nothing else in the file; placement verifies the render as it verifies `.claude.json`. |
| Codex | `type: "mcp_tool"` hook handlers call a named tool on a configured MCP server with an `input` template whose `${field}` placeholders resolve from the event JSON (a whole-string placeholder yields the JSON value, a placeholder for a field the event lacks fails the handler silently, so templates are per event); `SessionEnd` cannot use `mcp_tool`. Trust (proven without any bypass flag against Codex 0.153.4): the executable's own app-server lists hooks with their `key` (`<hooks.json path>:<event>:<group>:<handler>`) and `currentHash` (`hooks/list` over stdio JSON-RPC after `initialize`), and the TUI's trust action writes `[hooks.state."<key>"] trusted_hash = "<currentHash>"` into the user `config.toml`; the host does the same at configuration time and all five hooks fired. The hash is `sha256:` over the canonical JSON of the TOML-normalized identity `{event_name, matcher, hooks: [handler with timeout filled]}` and reproduces host-side for tool events (`matcher = ""`); for events without matchers Codex drops the matcher, so the app-server answer is the authority. `omit_tools_from` hides `bee_hooks` from the model's tool list but does not stop a model call naming it: a scripted `function_call` of `hook` in namespace `mcp__bee_hooks` was executed, so omission is not authorization. What does differ is executable-set request metadata: hook-engine calls carry `_meta = {threadId, progressToken}`, model calls carry `_meta = {callId, "x-codex-turn-metadata": {...}}`; the gateway requires the former on the hook endpoint and refuses the latter, and records provenance as "executable metadata, version-pinned", not as verified hook origin. | A second server `[mcp_servers.bee_hooks]` in the provider file with `url = .../hook/{action}` (an MCP endpoint serving one tool, `hook`), `bearer_token_env_var = "BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN"` and `omit_tools_from = ["direct", "deferred", "code_mode"]`; `$CODEX_HOME/hooks.json` with the admitted events, one `mcp_tool` handler each (`server: bee_hooks, tool: hook, timeout: 2`) and a per-event `input` template naming only fields that event carries (`event`, `session_id`, then `turn_id`/`tool_name`/`tool_use_id`/`tool_input` for `PreToolUse`, `tool_response` for `PostToolUse`, `source` for `SessionStart`, `prompt` for `UserPromptSubmit`, `last_assistant_message` for `Stop`); `[hooks.state]` trust entries taken from the pinned executable's `hooks/list`. |

The hook credential is a second credential of the same binding (`kind = hook`), materialized by the same runner under the same key into `BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN`; the MCP credential cannot submit hooks and the hook credential cannot call tools (proven in `tests/gateway.py` on both endpoints). Codex request metadata is classification only, version-pinned validation of a shape the executable sets and never an authenticated origin: the MCP form refuses model-shaped, mixed and missing metadata and records `codex:hook_engine` as provenance, and no permission, settlement or privileged action may depend on it. The Codex trust procedure is version-pinned configuration compatibility: the host takes each hook's key and `currentHash` from the pinned executable's own app-server (`hooks/list`), writes them as `[hooks.state."<key>"] trusted_hash` into the rendered `config.toml`, and the acceptance binds that rendered configuration and the executable measurement together; a new executable version needs renewed validation.

## Contract

| Boundary | Rule |
|---|---|
| Identity | The binding admitted at open gains `hooks` (the admitted event names, from the launch policy's `gateway_hooks` once the carrier integrates). Attempt, action, thread, owner incarnation and carrier epoch come from the binding the presented hook credential resolves to; the payload's `session_id`, `cwd`, `transcript_path` and any id are recorded as the harness's claims, never used to route. An event outside the catalog is refused 400, one outside the binding's `hooks` 403. Occurrence identity, from the captures: `PreToolUse`/`PostToolUse`/`PostToolUseFailure` by `tool_use_id`; `UserPromptSubmit` by `prompt_id` (Claude Code) or `turn_id` (Codex); `SessionStart` by `session_id` plus `source`; `SessionEnd` by `session_id`. `Stop` and `StopFailure` carry no identifier of their own occurrence (a prompt may stop more than once), so every stop is ambiguous. An identical replay under one unambiguous occurrence answers the same event; a changed submission under it conflicts (409); an ambiguous event is recorded per delivery, reported `ambiguous` by the status query and never merged, so a lost reply followed by a replay yields two rows for it, which is the stated limitation. A harness retry that reuses a `tool_use_id` with different content conflicts rather than silently merging. Nothing here claims exactly-once delivery. |
| Authority | Hook submission needs the hook credential; MCP tools need the tool credential; neither implies the other. The gateway answers an http hook with 2xx and an empty body and an MCP hook with a bare text result, never with `decision`, `hookSpecificOutput`, `continue`, `additionalContext` or any field the harness could act on, so a hook can never block, alter or extend a turn; a decision field inside a submitted payload is dropped before recording and never reaches an answer or a control record. Hooks never create actions, attempts, approvals or grants, and `Stop`, `SessionEnd` and `StopFailure` never settle anything: settlement stays with the carrier's terminal envelope and exit. Timing is a separate matter from authority: hook delivery is synchronous in both harnesses and bounded by the configured 2 s timeout per hook, so an unreachable, slow or draining gateway delays a turn by at most that per hook and never stops it; the adapter proofs measure this for each harness. |
| Ordering | The HTTP handler and the `hook` tool submit to the current fenced carrier of the binding's attempt through the carrier's mailbox, tagged with the binding's carrier epoch; the carrier commits `observation` records of kind `bee.harness.hook` (`event`, `tool_name`, `tool_use_id`, bounded `tool_input`/`tool_response` digests and previews, `duration_ms`, harness claims) through its existing commit path and checkpoint. A submission for an epoch below the carrier's is refused as stale; no HTTP handler writes a record. |
| Retry | Event identity is `{binding_id, event, tool_use_id or prompt_id or session phase}` with a canonical digest of the bounded payload. An identical replay (same identity, same digest) is deduplicated to the same record and answered as committed; the same identity with a different digest is refused CONFLICT and recorded once as `hook.conflict`. |
| Acknowledgment | The carrier's mailbox is memory only, so 202 means volatile acceptance: the submission may still be lost with the carrier. Only the carrier's durable commit earns 200 committed. The harness never sees these bodies (http answers are empty, MCP answers a bare text); a status query `GET /hook/{action}/{event_id}` (hook credential) answers `queued`, `committed` or `unknown`, where `unknown` after a loss permits replay under the same event identity and the replay commits once. A replacement carrier recovers committed identities from the durable records before accepting submissions and fences acknowledgments the old carrier gave. |
| Lifetime | Hooks are accepted while the binding is valid and the attempt is live under the current carrier. Carrier replacement: submissions inherit the binding like tool calls and go to the new carrier; a hook queued in a lost carrier is lost (it was never acknowledged committed) and the harness's replay recommits it. Settlement: hooks after the carrier ended the turn are recorded as `late` observations only while the attempt is open; after receipt they are refused. Expiry or revocation refuses at once, as for tools. Drain: queued hooks are committed before the deadline, new ones refused with 503 past it. |
| Bounds | Payload 32 KiB and 64 queued per binding; over that the gateway answers 429 with `Retry-After` and a replay of an already queued event still answers. Records carry allowlisted normalized fields only, never the payload: `event`, the occurrence, the harness claims `session_id`/`turn_id`/`prompt_id`/`tool_use_id`/`agent_id` (validated identifiers), the selected values only as enumerated codes (`source`, `reason`, `permission_mode`, `error`, `notification_type` against the closed lists in `bee.gateway:hooks`), booleans and numbers (`stop_hook_active`, `duration_ms`) and `tool_name` as a validated bounded identifier; any value outside its enumeration or shape is omitted, since `reason` or `error` may carry output, paths or secrets. For the content fields (`tool_input`, `tool_response`, `prompt`, `last_assistant_message`, `message`, `error_details`, `cwd`, `transcript_path`) only the byte length and a sha256 over the canonical JSON of that one field are kept, whose sole purpose is to tell an identical replay from a changed submission under the same occurrence; a digest establishes neither occurrence identity nor confidentiality. No preview, no content capture by default; a future opt-in needs a host policy and disclosure. Control fields (`decision`, `continue`, `stopReason`, `hookSpecificOutput`, `additionalContext`, `systemMessage`, ...) are dropped before normalization and never reach a record or an answer. |

## Proof order (Astra round 62) and where each stands

1. Codex hook execution with supported trust configuration and omitted tools: proven with the executable's app-server hashes and no bypass flag, through the gateway's MCP hook endpoint, in `gateway_harness_test` (combined runtime).
2. Explicit model invocation and cross-credential denial: `tests/gateway.py` proves the tool credential is refused on both hook endpoints and the hook credential on the tool endpoint, that no thread tool is served on the hook endpoint, and that model-shaped, mixed and missing metadata are refused; the model's ability to name the omitted tool is recorded above, which is why classification is not authorization.
3. Claude http hook behavior: measured against a stub (slow, 500, absent, decision); through the gateway, `gateway_harness_test` proves five hooks arrive under the hook credential with empty answers while the read completes, bounded in time.
4. Occurrence identity, replay and ambiguity: `tests/gateway.py` proves identical replay, changed-content conflict, repeated Stop replay, ambiguous SessionStart per delivery, the payload and queue bounds, 429 with `Retry-After`, and revocation refusing at once; `hooks_test` proves the pure rules.
5. Carrier integration and durable acknowledgment: done. `gateway_carrier_test` proves the fixture child's hooks become `bee.harness.hook` records with no content and every queue row committed after the runner's seal; a crash after the thread commit and before the acknowledgment (step `hooks_committed`) is recovered by the replacement carrier with exactly one record per occurrence; two live carriers on one queue, the original held after its claim or after its commit, end with the replacement's commit and acknowledgment only, the original refused by the thread or idle and fenced; a carrier held after the attempt starts lets the child fill the queue to its bound and receive 429, and once it continues every accepted row ends committed. `gateway_harness_test` proves the same through placement with the real harnesses: Claude Code from the settings adapter written by the runner, Codex from `hooks.json` and the `bee` profile layer carrying the host-computed trust state (`--profile bee` on the launch line), both with the hook credential the runner materialized into `BEE_GATEWAY_HOOK_TOKEN`.

Also carried: a `Stop` hook does not end the turn and a missing `Stop` (SIGTERM, API error) does not delay settlement; a hook carrying a decision is recorded without it and answered without it; drain and revocation refuse; a 64-deep queue answers 429 and records one overload; no record carries a payload beyond the allowlisted fields.
